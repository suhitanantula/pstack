# Participant Rhythm

## Entry condition

Every participant enters with:

- a real strategic challenge;
- authority or meaningful access to work on it;
- enough context to identify stakeholders and constraints;
- agreement about what information may be used in the course environment.

The intake produces a **Strategic Learning Brief**, not an automated admission score.

## Step 1 — Perceive

**Participant question:** What is actually happening, and what are we not seeing?

The agent helps:

- map actors, evidence, tensions and uncertainty;
- distinguish the stated problem from the problem behind it;
- surface weak signals and contradictions;
- identify where human, political or relational context is missing.

The participant contributes:

- tacit context;
- stakes and values;
- what must be preserved;
- which interpretations feel consequential or wrong.

**Artefact:** Situation Map and Inquiry Agenda.

**Learning evidence:** the participant can distinguish evidence, interpretation and uncertainty.

## Step 2 — Perform

**Participant question:** What is the smallest intentional move that lets reality answer us?

The agent helps:

- turn insight into a bounded experiment or intervention;
- define what done and useful mean;
- execute or simulate within agreed boundaries;
- document what happened.

The participant contributes:

- strategic intent;
- trust and irreversibility judgements;
- permission boundaries;
- the meaning of “good enough.”

**Artefact:** Live Move Brief and Execution Evidence.

**Learning evidence:** the participant can move without turning speed into unexamined automation.

## Step 3 — Portfolio

**Participant question:** What options become visible now that we have acted and learned?

The agent helps:

- generate safe, bold and hybrid paths;
- make options tangible through prototypes or worked artefacts;
- compare assumptions and failure modes;
- preserve worthwhile optionality.

The participant contributes:

- taste and strategic preference;
- risk appetite;
- contextual fit;
- which options deserve attention.

**Artefact:** Live Portfolio of Plays.

**Learning evidence:** the participant can maintain disciplined optionality without avoiding recommendation.

## Step 4 — Pause

**Participant question:** What should we promote, preserve or stop—and why?

The agent helps:

- expose parameters and assumptions;
- compare consequences and risks;
- make the kill list explicit;
- record when and why the decision should be revisited.

The participant contributes:

- ethical and cultural judgement;
- accountability;
- timing and risk decisions;
- the final recommendation.

**Artefact:** Judgement and Decision Record.

**Learning evidence:** the participant can make a transparent bet without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

## Step 5 — Progress

**Participant question:** What do we know now that should change the next loop?

The agent helps:

- compare expectation and outcome;
- synthesise evidence;
- separate model, problem and process lessons;
- detect patterns across cycles.

The participant contributes:

- meaning-making;
- redefinition of success;
- what the evidence should change;
- what capability they are developing.

**Artefact:** Learning and Next-Loop Brief.

**Learning evidence:** the participant can convert action into compounding improvement.

## The loop

Progress feeds the next Perceive. Any step can trigger a return:

- Perform can reveal that the framing was wrong.
- Portfolio can expose missing context.
- Pause can preserve an option rather than kill it.
- Progress can change the problem entirely.

The learning objective is not memorising five labels. It is developing the judgement to recognise which mode of work is needed now.

## Course arc

| Moment | Participant experience | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Before joining | Five-step intake around a live challenge | Strategic Learning Brief |
| Opening | Activate PStack and establish privacy boundaries | Participant workspace |
| Early course | Perceive and make one Perform move | Situation Map and evidence |
| Middle | Build Portfolio and exercise Pause | Options and decision record |
| Completion | Run Progress and articulate changed practice | Strategic intervention and next-loop brief |
| 30 days later | Reuse PStack independently | Continuation evidence |

## Facilitator role

The facilitator does not compete with the agents. The facilitator:

- helps participants recognise which P they are really in;
- challenges weak evidence and convenient framing;
- protects human accountability;
- notices when the stack is producing fluency without learning;
- supports reflection across participants without exposing private material.
